How I’m approaching it

Give people a clear place to contribute.

I restructured SSD into departments so responsibility could be clearer as the organization grew. Leading a club at this scale means thinking about the officers doing the work as well as the students who attend.

I want someone joining SSD to find a useful next step: practice a problem, learn something in a workshop, meet a speaker, or find people to build with. The structure behind those experiences should make them easier to deliver consistently.

That is also why I’ve been writing and refining officer responsibilities. A title is a starting point. People need to understand what they own, who they work with, and what a useful contribution looks like.

A current planning focus

Stronger industry relationships

I’ve been planning an Industry Relations department with two teams under one department head. Sponsorship outreach and speaker relationships need distinct ownership, even though both depend on thoughtful follow-through.

What I’m working toward

More continuity.
More ways in.

  1. Make technical growth approachable.

    Keep coding practice, technical workshops, and career preparation connected to clear next steps for students at different experience levels.

  2. Build relationships that last.

    Give outreach a consistent follow-up rhythm, with shared context that officers can carry into the next conversation.

  3. Leave better handoffs.

    Document roles, decisions, and working processes so the next team can keep improving the club rather than reconstructing everything.

These are the priorities I’m developing. Specific events and the next semester’s schedule will take shape with the team.
