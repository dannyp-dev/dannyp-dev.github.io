Turning relation notation into something I can reason about

Ordered pairs, arrows, and the meaning behind a short expression like aRb.

Danny Perojevic · October 13, 2026· Updated October 15, 2026

What does aRb mean?

I stopped during my relations work to ask what aRa and aRb actually mean. That question seems small until an entire proof depends on it. The statement aRb says that (a, b) belongs to the relation R. It does not tell me what the relationship is until I read the definition of R.

Try a relation I already know

Take divisibility on the positive integers. In that relation, 2R6 means that 2 divides 6. It does not mean that 6 divides 2. For subset inclusion, BRC means B is a subset of C. Reading the notation as a sentence makes the direction harder to lose.

The loop in aRa

An arrow from a to b is a useful picture of aRb. The expression aRa becomes a loop at a. Reflexivity requires that loop for every element of the underlying set, including elements that do not appear in any other pair. That last detail matters when checking a claim about the whole set.

How I’m using this

I have been keeping a short symbolic argument next to a plain-language translation. For example, symmetry takes aRb to bRa: reverse this pair. If I cannot say the step that plainly, I go back to the definition before adding another line. It slows the first attempt down, but I can see where I lost the argument.
